First put in your own numbers. Then change the settings and see what happens to cost, value, time and risk. The model runs 300 times after every change, so you see a range of possible results, not one answer.

What these settings mean

    Three things to try

    1. Find a setting where you get your money back within three years. What did you have to give up? Would your board accept that?
    2. Leave everything else where it is. Move the gate slider slowly from one end to the other. The result gets worse at both ends. Where are your gates today, and which way are you moving?
    3. Give more of the team to new ideas, then set protected time to zero. Explain to the group why the extra money bought almost nothing.
    This model is a tool for discussion, not a prediction. It assumes that money only comes from ideas that reach launch, that the number of people, not the budget, limits how much can run at the same time, and that unprotected time gets taken back by normal work. Change the numbers at the top of the page until the model argues with you honestly. The three sizes of idea used here — small improvement, extension, and completely new — are usually called core, adjacent and transformational.
